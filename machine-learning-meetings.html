MEETING 01 / UNIT 06

Meeting 1 — Kickoff

Project approach

  • Selected Track 1 classical machine learning, initially focusing on factors affecting Airbnb prices.
  • Considered enriching the approximately 48,000-listing dataset with NYC open data, subject to lecturer approval.
  • Agreed to propose business questions and vote through Telegram, and work asynchronously in parallel.

Team process and decisions

  • Julio established a GitHub Projects Kanban board for tasks and meeting tickets.
  • One reviewer per pull request, with a target three-day review turnaround; work should continue without unnecessary blocking.
  • Weekly meetings at the same time; the next meeting was planned for Saturday 15 August.
  • Meeting evidence to be kept as transcripts and summaries, without recordings.
  • Ask the lecturer whether external datasets and comparison across tracks were permitted.
  • Draft a long-form report before reducing it to the final word limit.

MEETING 02 / UNIT 06

Meeting 2 — Baseline and work allocation

Progress and model review

  • Reviewed and merged Julio’s Random Forest baseline pull request, incorporating points of interest and subway-distance variables.
  • Observed that high-price deviations were widespread rather than concentrated in expected neighbourhoods.
  • Preferred tuning a suitable model rather than attempting indiscriminate comparisons.
  • Zee identified the need for an evaluation method within the notebook.

Tasks and team process

  • John-Jon to develop visualisations tied to notebook versions and hashes, including outputs in PR descriptions.
  • Zee to draft report outline and research supporting literature.
  • Julio to create a Google Doc in the shared Drive; dataset cleaning marked complete.
  • Patrick to add notes and arrange the next meeting.
  • Coding targeted for completion before drafting, visualisation integration and a Turnitin sandbox check.
  • Weekly meeting facilitation to rotate across the team.

MEETING 03 / UNIT 06

Meeting 3 — Clustering review and revised question

My contribution: K-means clustering

  • I presented three clusters with listing counts, average prices, profiles and geographic distributions.
  • The team interpreted geographically overlapping clusters as market segments rather than distinct neighbourhood zones.
  • My clustering PR #26 was reviewed; requested changes included moving imports to the beginning, running pre-commit and calculating silhouette score on the full dataset rather than a 5,000-row sample.
  • I was assigned to address review comments, prepare the branch for integration, and draft the methodology section.

Validation and business question

  • Reviewed Julio’s PR #27 replacing in-sample residuals with five-fold out-of-fold predictions.
  • Added an NTA-and-room-type median-price baseline as a comparison for the Random Forest.
  • Removed review-derived features for the proposed cold-start use case, since new listings lack reviews.
  • Reframed from identifying allegedly overpriced listings to estimating a starting asking price and geographic pricing volatility.
  • Agreed that residual error reflects uncertainty and unobserved characteristics, not proof that an individual host has mispriced a listing.
  • PR integration might require review, merge ordering or rebasing.

Writing ownership

  • Julio: data preparation; Džemal: methodology; John-Jon: results and visualisations; Zee: business recommendations.
  • Patrick was proposed for introduction and conclusion, pending confirmation.
  • Targeted merged code, most graphics and a complete initial draft by the end of the following week.

MEETING 04 / UNIT 06

Meeting 4 — Final writing and scope

Progress and decisions

  • With roughly one week left, further deep code review was deprioritised in favour of writing and graphics.
  • The business question was reworded to avoid the word “strictly”, since features included reviews and availability as well as spatial information.
  • The outline and most sections were drafted, with a conclusion still outstanding.
  • Five visualisations were selected for the main report from the shared Drive.
  • Planned to unify tone and formatting after completing the content and add remaining supporting literature.

Actions

  • Clean the code and submit the visualisation PR.
  • Draft the conclusion and conduct final styling and clarity checks.
  • Prepare the report for Turnitin and a midweek catch-up on Wednesday 2 September.
  • Target submission the following weekend.

MEETING 05 / UNIT 06

Meeting 5 — Discrepancies and final-model positioning

Quality assurance findings

  • A strict cold-start claim conflicted with features that required existing review history, including number of reviews and reviews per month.
  • A cited $47.34 baseline MAE and 9.6% improvement were not reproducible from the latest PDF.
  • The verified Random Forest MAE was $42.79; the notebook and visuals needed a full rerun to reconcile outputs.

Decisions and actions

  • Dropped the strict cold-start framing in favour of a full-featured price model more suitable for established listings.
  • Merge the visuals PR, move imports to the top, and rerun the notebook before circulating the refreshed PDF.
  • Update the report, conclusion and notebook business question consistently.
  • Use Turnitin sandbox, resolve comments, check styling and exclude an appendix.

MEETING 06 / UNIT 06

Meeting 6 — Submission preparation

Submission and repository

  • Submit the report after a Turnitin sandbox check.
  • Provide a PDF copy of the main notebook alongside the report for assessment access.
  • Resolve remaining PR conflicts and rerun the relevant work before submission.
  • Add the GitHub repository address beneath the module name on the cover page.
  • Plan to make the repository public after the submission deadline and request tutor access if needed.

Formatting and final checks

  • Use Arial 12 consistently throughout the report.
  • Alphabetise references and correct spacing and formatting inconsistencies.
  • Complete a final review of both report and notebook before submission.
